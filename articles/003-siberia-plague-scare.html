A plague scare in Siberia tests Russia's credibility

The Economist — 2026-10-15

P1 THE RUMOURS that emerged sounded like the opening of a thriller. A young woman working in a Siberian research lab supposedly breaks a test tube containing a lethal plague pathogen. She is dead within 48 hours. Authorities race to contain the situation. The questions outnumber the answers, breeding suggestions of conspiracy. The fears may prove unfounded: there may not have been a leak at Russia’s Irkutsk Anti-Plague Research Institute. But the world can be forgiven for its panicked interest.

P2 Some facts are not under dispute. Darya Shipilova, a 28-year-old researcher at the institute, died at a regional hospital on October 2nd. Around 200 of Ms Shipilova’s contacts have been placed under medical observation, suggesting authorities believe she may have died of an infectious disease. But no cause of death has been officially confirmed, according to the World Health Organisation (WHO). Russia’s federal health watchdog claimed to have found “no evidence of microorganisms related to her professional activities”, and said the cause of death was “pneumonia of unknown etiology”.

P3 Beyond confirming the death Russian officials have provided little information, though they are urgently implementing regional control measures. On Monday Marco Rubio, America’s secretary of state, said that his government is “watching and monitoring it closely”. With the covid pandemic still a recent memory, it is no wonder the world has shown close interest. Inevitably, some have asked if the incident is connected to Russia’s biological warfare programme, which Western intelligence services say is still active.

P4 The bacterium Yersinia pestis, generically known as “plague”, manifests in two forms. Every year thousands of cases of bubonic plague occur naturally when people catch it after contact with wildlife such as rodents. Bubonic plague is treatable with antibiotics if caught early, but untreated it can become pneumonic plague, which can be transmitted via respiratory droplets. This is harder to treat and has a higher mortality rate. Legitimate reasons exist for study of the pathogen, such as to understand how it causes disease, and to develop vaccines and treatments.

P5 But Filippa Lentzos, a biosecurity expert at King’s College London, says the lack of transparency around the incident in Siberia is worrying. Plague is one of a group of high-risk pathogens that have easy transmissibility and high mortality rates, including anthrax, smallpox, and viral hemorrhagic fevers such as Ebola. Biosafety laboratories are proliferating around the world, raising the likelihood of accidents.

P6 These pathogens are also considered prime candidates for use in bioweapons. Plague can be aerosolised to ease transmission to humans and genetically modified to make it resistant to antibiotics. Despite ratifying the Biological Weapons Convention in 1975, the Soviet Union ran an extensive covert bioweapons programme. In 1992 Boris Yeltsin, then president of Russia, acknowledged the programme and agreed to dismantle it. But more recently Russia has maintained secrecy around its military biological-research facilities. Satellite imagery of one such site outside Moscow indicates significant expansion since Russia invaded Ukraine in 2022. In 2023 a report on treaty compliance by America’s State Department concluded that Russia maintains an offensive biological weapons programme.

P7 Based on the unofficial information currently available on the situation in Irkutsk, the WHO believes the risk to the general population to be low. No evidence suggests that the institute in Irkutsk is linked to bioweapons activities, and Russia insists its cloaked military sites only undertake defensive research. But Russia said the same thing about its chemical-weapons research before it attempted to assassinate Sergei Skripal, a Russian defector in Britain, with a military-grade nerve poison in 2018. There may be nothing to the lab-leak rumours. But with so little transparency, it is hard to take Russia at its word.

主句The questions outnumber the answers —— 主句：问题比答案多（outnumber = 在数量上超过）

长难句解析 · ANALYSIS

Russia’s federal health watchdog claimed to have found “no evidence of microorganisms related to her professional activities”, and said the cause of death was “pneumonia of unknown etiology”.

主句Russia’s federal health watchdog claimed ... and said ... —— 并列谓语：声称……并称……

Inevitably, some have asked if the incident is connected to Russia’s biological warfare programme, which Western intelligence services say is still active.

PARA 4 · 讲解
参考译文 · TRANSLATION

耶尔森氏菌（俗称“鼠疫”）有两种形态。每年有数千例腺鼠疫在自然条件下发生，人们因接触啮齿类等野生动物而感染。腺鼠疫若早期发现，可用抗生素治疗；但若不加治疗，可能发展为肺鼠疫，后者可通过飞沫传播。它更难治疗，死亡率也更高。研究这一病原体有正当理由，例如弄清其致病机理，以及研发疫苗与疗法。

本段词汇 · WORDS
pathogenn.病原体
💡 形容词 pathogenic 致病的；医学英语核心词
bubonic plaguen.phr.腺鼠疫
💡 bubonic 腺体的；对应 pneumonic plague 肺鼠疫
treatableadj.可治疗的
💡 -able 后缀：treatable / curable / preventable
mortality raten.phr.死亡率
💡 区分：mortality 死亡率 / morbidity 发病率
respiratory dropletn.phr.飞沫（呼吸道飞沫）
💡 respiratory /rəˈspɪrətri/；covid 语境高频词
长难句解析 · ANALYSIS

Bubonic plague is treatable with antibiotics if caught early, but untreated it can become pneumonic plague, which can be transmitted via respiratory droplets.

主句Bubonic plague is treatable ... but (it) can become pneumonic plague —— 转折并列的两个主句
从句if caught early = if it is caught early；untreated = if it is untreated —— 状语从句省略主语 + be 的典型
从句which can be transmitted via respiratory droplets —— 非限制性定语从句，说明传播途径
考点科技英语高频：状语从句省略（if + 过去分词 / 形容词）。写作中模仿可显著提升句式密度。

Despite ratifying the Biological Weapons Convention in 1975, the Soviet Union ran an extensive covert bioweapons programme.

参考译文 · TRANSLATION

根据目前关于伊尔库茨克局势的非官方信息，世卫组织认为普通民众面临的风险较低。没有证据显示伊尔库茨克研究所与生物武器活动有关联，俄罗斯坚称其秘密军事设施只进行防御性研究。但 2018 年俄罗斯叛逃者谢尔盖·斯克里帕尔在英国遭军用级神经毒剂暗杀未遂之前，俄罗斯对化学武器研究也说过同样的话。实验室泄漏的谣言或许空穴来风。但在如此缺乏透明度的情况下，很难轻信俄罗斯的一面之词。

Background

Context that helps you understand the article.

鼠疫与耶尔森氏菌

鼠疫（plague）由耶尔森氏菌（Yersinia pestis）引起，常见宿主为啮齿类动物。它有两种主要形态：腺鼠疫（bubonic plague）经跳蚤叮咬传播，早期可用抗生素治愈；肺鼠疫（pneumonic plague）侵犯肺部，可经飞沫人传人，死亡率更高。世卫组织将鼠疫列为重新出现的传染病，全球每年仍有数千例报告。

《生物武器公约》与核查困境

《生物武器公约》（BWC）1972 年签署、1975 年生效，禁止研发生物与毒素武器。但它没有强制核查机制，履约主要靠缔约国自查与情报判断——这正是文中“缺乏透明度”争论的制度根源。文章提到的斯克里帕尔事件（2018 年英国索尔兹伯里神经毒剂案）常被西方国家用作质疑俄方履约诚意的例证。

CHOICE

What does the article say about the cause of Darya Shipilova’s death?

Explanation: 第二段明确说：据 WHO，死因尚未官方证实（no cause of death has been officially confirmed）；俄方仅称“病因不明的肺炎”。A、B 与原文矛盾；D 属无中生有的阴谋论猜测。
CHOICE

The word “unfounded” (Paragraph 1) is closest in meaning to ______.

Explanation: un-（否定）+ found（建立）+ -ed：没有建立在事实之上的，即“毫无根据的”。原文 The fears may prove unfounded 意为“担忧可能被证明毫无根据”。
CHOICE

Why is the lack of transparency around the incident worrying, according to the article?

Explanation: 第五、六段逻辑链：高危病原体（传播性强、死亡率高）+ 实验室激增 = 事故概率上升；且此类病原体是生物武器首选材料。A 偷换概念（早期可治）；C 与原文相反（WHO 在评估）；D 无中生有（俄未退约，而是被质疑履约）。
FILL IN

Despite ______ (ratify) the Biological Weapons Convention in 1975, the Soviet Union ran an extensive covert bioweapons programme.

Explanation: despite 是介词，后接名词或动名词，故用 ratifying。若要用从句，需改写为 Although the Soviet Union ratified ... 。这也是本文最值得背诵的让步句式。
