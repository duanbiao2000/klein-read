Are exams evil?

The Economist — 2026-10-08

P1 At times the words feel almost random. The 1928 paper offered “Time”; the 1956 one “Islands”; 2006, “Water”. At other times the single-word essay titles that All Souls College, Oxford put in its entrance exams might have felt more pertinent. In 1990, for instance, the paper offered students—or perhaps demanded of them—“Memory”. Sometimes the exam was preeningly self-referential. In 1952 it asked students to write about “Oxford”; in 1948 it stylishly offered the single word “Style” on an otherwise almost blank page. Less stylishly, it repeated that in 2005, which rather lacked “Originality” (1932).

P2 The All Souls entrance exam, which this year finished on October 1st, has been called the “hardest exam in the world” and “the SAS of academia”. Each year over 100 students, most of them recent graduates of Oxford, sit its four papers (two general, two specialist), each lasting three hours, over the course of two days. Two students alone will win the glittering prize of a seven-year fellowship at the college. Depending on your view, the exam is either a demanding tool to distinguish “Genius” (1935) or an intellectual “Error” (1993).

P3 A similar dichotomy dogs the college. It is variously seen as either the brainiest and grandest of the grand and brainy Oxbridge colleges, or as a snooty backwater where “the best brains of Oxford are preserved—in alcohol”. Whereas other colleges churn out celebrities (Boris Johnson went to Balliol) or are celebrities themselves (Christ Church starred in “Harry Potter”), All Souls is more recondite. A sign in its porters’ lodge informs “Harry Potter Fans” that they have “come to the wrong place”, tacitly implying that to have done so is sheer “Vulgarity” (1947). Cambridge dons used to refer to All Souls as “Arseholes”, which is yet to be used as an essay title.

P4 Much of the college’s mystique comes from its exam. This is ironic, because its intention was the opposite. In the mid-Victorian era, British intellectuals were seized by the novel idea that brains, not breeding, should determine success. Britain, in other words, should be run by an intellectual aristocracy chosen by open competition, not an actual one. One result of this “Revolution” (1926) was the All Souls exam. Another was the entrance test for the Indian civil service. The descendants of both, to this day, are called “the hardest exam in the world”. And though created with egalitarian ideals in mind, both have been accused of “Prejudice” (1961).

P5 Not without cause. To read the All Souls papers is to take soil samples of antique attitudes. The 1857 paper asks testily about the “chief obstacles” to the “civilisation” of India. The 1866 exam demanded an essay on “The Government of Subject races”. There are moments of self-awareness: the 1871 paper asked students about “the effect of the spirit of their age upon standard histories”. By the 1980s that spirit had changed: 1987’s paper offered “Race”.

P6 However, much of the hype around the exam is mere mythology, and the college has made efforts to demystify it. In 2010 it ditched the one-word paper (partly because it was forbidding; mostly because it was bad at differentiating candidates). This pleased almost no one: now the exam is simultaneously accused of favouring the middle class and of wokery. Though that could, of course, be down to “Envy” (1998). Whatever the changes, the exam undoubtedly remains formidable. Shortlisted candidates are cross-examined by around 70 college fellows in their gowns. They are “draped”, says Armand D’Angour, a professor at Jesus College who took the exam in 1983, “around the room like vampire bats”.

P7 A better question than “Is it woke” is “Does it work?” Does it produce the “intellectual aristocracy” that it was meant to? The list of its alumni drawn from the past 500 years is undoubtedly impressive. It includes Christopher Wren, Isaiah Berlin and, in the modern day, Sir John Vickers, an economist, and Katherine Rundell, a children’s writer. Then again, other colleges have produced far bigger hitters. A far-from-complete list of impressive alumni from nearby Balliol College, for instance, includes Adam Smith, Herbert Asquith and Aldous Huxley.

P8 A more serious problem is that passing the All Souls exam offers bright scholars seven years of idleness, since their prize fellowships come with almost no strings attached. This may be less a blessing than a curse. All Souls skims the cream from other Oxford colleges (historically, only those with the very best first-class degrees were encouraged to sit it) then lets it curdle. Its atmosphere, wrote A.N. Wilson, a historian, was that of a “club more than a college” and the “ruin of many of the fellows”. The 1938 All Souls paper wondered “Are examinations an evil?” A better question might have been: is this one?

P9 Perhaps. But then what, as the 2025 general paper asked, “is regret good for?” The All Souls exam might be flawed and it might suffer from “Bias” (2003). But it is also magnificent fun, pondering such things as: “What sort of book might Satan write?” (2024). Which surely brings everyone a little “Happiness” (1953).

全灵学院的入学考试（今年于 10 月 1 日结束）被称为“世上最难的考试”和“学术界的 SAS 特种部队”。每年有 100 多名学生——多为牛津应届毕业生——参加它的四份试卷（两份综合、两份专业），每份三小时，共考两天。最终只有两人能赢得那耀眼的奖赏：学院的七年研究员职位。视你的看法而定，这场考试要么是甄别“天才”（1935 年考题）的严苛工具，要么本身就是一场智力“错误”（1993 年考题）。

一种类似的二元对立也纠缠着这所学院。它时而被视为牛剑众多显赫学院中最聪明、最气派的一所；时而又被看作势利的死水一潭——“牛津最优秀的大脑都保存在这里——用酒精保存”。别的学院盛产名人（鲍里斯·约翰逊出自贝利奥尔学院），或自己就是名人（基督堂学院在《哈利·波特》里出镜），全灵学院则要冷僻深奥得多。学院门房里挂着一块告示，告知“哈利·波特粉丝们”他们“来错了地方”，言下之意是来错了地方纯属 1947 年考题所说的“庸俗”。剑桥的学究们过去常拿谐音把全灵学院叫作“Arseholes”（粗话谐音），这词还从未被用作作文题。

参考译文 · TRANSLATION

这所学院的神秘感很大程度上来自它的考试。这颇具讽刺意味，因为考试的初衷恰恰相反。维多利亚时代中期，英国知识界被一个新颖观念攫住：决定成败的应当是才智，而非出身。换言之，英国应当由一场公开竞选出的“知识贵族”来治理，而不是真正的世袭贵族。这场“革命”（1926 年考题）的一个结果就是全灵考试；另一个结果是印度文官系统的入学考试。两者的后裔至今仍被称为“世上最难的考试”。而且，尽管都怀揣 1961 年考题所说的“平等”理想而生，两者也都被指控犯有“偏见”（同样是 1961 年考题）。

也许吧。但话说回来——2025 年综合卷问得好——“后悔有什么好处？”全灵考试可能有缺陷，也可能患有 2003 年考题所说的“偏见”。但它也妙趣横生，让人思考诸如“撒旦会写一本什么样的书？”（2024 年考题）这样的问题。这想必给每个人都带来了一点 1953 年考题所说的“幸福”。

But it is also magnificent fun, pondering such things as: “What sort of book might Satan write?” (2024).

主句it is also magnificent fun —— 主句：它也妙趣横生（it is fun + 名词化表达）

Background

Context that helps you understand the article.

全灵学院与“世上最难的考试”

全灵学院（All Souls College）1438 年由亨利六世创建，是牛津唯一一所以研究为主的学院：没有本科生，只有研究员（fellows）。其入学考试（ Examination Fellowship）创设于 1857 年，每年从 100 多名应考者中录取 0-2 人，获七年研究员职位。标志性的一词作文题（1932-2009 年）只给一个词（如“Style”“Memory”），要求写一篇结构完整的论文，被戏称为“学术界的 SAS”。2010 年起改为选题作文。

文化背景：“知识贵族”与印度文官考试

维多利亚时代中期，英国推动“凭才智而非出身”的用人革命：1854 年《麦考利报告》后建立的印度文官考试（ICS）与全灵考试并称两大“最难考试”，都试图以公开竞争选拔精英，也都长期被批评带有阶层、种族偏见。文中反复出现的括号标题是历年考题——作者拿它们当双关词用（如缺乏“Originality”、被“Bias”困扰），读出这层幽默是理解本文文风的关键。

Useful Expressions

You can reuse these in exam writing.

Depending on your view

视你的看法而定

📌 呈现争议的中立开场：Depending on your view, X is either A or B.

churn out

大量炮制（略带贬义）

📌 churn out celebrities/graduates/books 批量产出名人/毕业生/书

To do A is to do B

做 A 就是做 B（定义/比喻句式）

📌 下定义一击即中：To read the papers is to take soil samples of antique attitudes.

less a blessing than a curse

与其说是福，不如说是祸

📌 less A than B 结构；评价事物双刃剑

come with no strings attached

不附带任何条件

📌 福利/机会类话题高频：prize fellowships come with almost no strings attached

skim the cream (from)

挑走最优秀的人或物

📌 教育/人才话题：All Souls skims the cream from other Oxford colleges.

CHOICE

Why was the one-word essay paper dropped in 2010?

Explanation: 第六段括号内说得很清楚：部分是因为它令人生畏（forbidding），主要是因为它难以区分考生水平（bad at differentiating candidates）。A 时间线错误（2010 年废除，非 1928 起不变）；C 是废止后出现的另一种指责；D 张冠李戴——2005 年重复的是“Style”（1948），而“Originality”是 1932 年考题，作者拿来讽刺这次重复。
CHOICE

The word “dichotomy” (Paragraph 3) is closest in meaning to ______.

Explanation: dichotomy = 二分法、截然对立。文中指对学院的两种对立评价：要么是“最聪明最显赫”，要么是“势利的死水潭”。动词 dog 在此意为“纠缠、困扰”。
CHOICE

What is the “more serious problem” with the exam, according to the article?

Explanation: 第八段：更严重的问题是七年“无附加条件”的闲散——“less a blessing than a curse”，甚至“the ruin of many of the fellows”。A、B 是文中提到的其他争议/玩笑；D 是第七段的对比，非“更严重的问题”。
FILL IN

Their prize fellowships come with almost no strings ______ (attach).

Explanation: no strings attached 固定搭配：“无附加条件”。attached 过去分词作后置修饰，come with sth attached 结构（附带某物而来）。
