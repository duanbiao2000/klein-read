📖 GUIDE

从澡堂热评到深夜秀式“TBPN”，从 All-In 峰会到 Dwarkesh 长谈——超级科技播客已成硅谷新权贵话筒。但文章指出：它们共享一种近乎近亲繁殖的圈内神秘感，是回音室，也是“单极文化”的推手；对圈外世界传递 AI 热情的努力并不奏效。学习重点：power law、watershed、echo chamber 等主题词，lap it up、sour grapes 等地道表达，以及 not only...so do 倒装等句式。

Superstar podcasts are Silicon Valley’s way of talking to itself

The Economist — 2026-10-08

P1 EACH WEEKDAY morning Jordi Hays and John Coogan, two entrepreneurs-turned-podcasters, enter a sauna in Los Angeles with copies of the Wall Street Journal. They come out with jackets, ties and hot takes on the tech news of the day. Then they go on air as hosts of the “Technology Business Programming Network” (“TBPN”) podcast, riffing with guests, banging gongs and blowing airhorns. The president of TBPN, Dylan Abruscato, calls it a “variety show” in the vein of a late-night talk show. Silicon Valley laps it up. It is not uncommon to see “TBPN”, rather than CNBC, streaming on the TV screens in tech firms’ offices.

P2 Podcasts are the latest example of a power law in Silicon Valley. Only a few become big hitters. Those that do attract the demigods of tech as guests, making them even more influential. “TBPN” has interviewed Sam Altman of OpenAI and Mark Zuckerberg of Meta. The “All-In” Podcast, hosted by four plutocrats who straddle tech and MAGA politics, held a summit in Los Angeles in September attended by Elon Musk and Jensen Huang. (When Donald Trump phoned Nvidia’s boss on stage during the summit, Mr Huang put the president on speakerphone.) Dwarkesh Patel, a bearded 26-year-old who acts—and looks—like a high priest of AI research, hosts tech bigwigs for hours on the “Dwarkesh Podcast”. So does Lex Fridman, a gravel-voiced computer scientist whose podcast runs longer than some marathons.

P3 All share an in-crowd mystique bordering on incestuousness. In April OpenAI reportedly spent $100m-200m to buy then-18-month-old TBPN (though there was no mention of the affiliation during Mr Altman’s interview). The “All-In” hosts are self-described “besties” with many of their billionaire interviewees. In San Francisco Mr Patel has shared digs with Anthropic’s researchers. They are fascinated by AI, which they talk about with passionate intensity and expertise. They are also an echo chamber, with a tin ear at times to the concerns of the rest of America.

P4 This new podcasting elite reflects a big change in Silicon Valley’s relationship with the media. During the last great investment boom, the dotcom era of the 1990s, publications like Wired were tech-industry gospels. They covered the internet like Rolling Stone wrote about rock’n’roll, says John Battelle, a founding editor of Wired: “We thought we were covering the counterculture.”

P5 But the rise of social media and the growing power and wealth of big tech generated a rift between Silicon Valley and news outlets. Mr Battelle says that parts of the tech establishment started to view traditional media as “anti-American” for questioning the “dominance of the tech hierarchy”. For their part, old media, whose business model has been hurt by the tech giants, sometimes smell of sour grapes. Podcasters, who share their guests’ techno-optimism, provide a friendlier platform.

P6 The watershed came during the 2024 presidential race. Led by Mr Trump, politicians saw the medium as a less filtered way of getting their message out than the press or broadcast TV. “All-In” played a role. Two of its billionaire co-hosts, David Sacks and Chamath Palihapitiya, threw a fundraiser in San Francisco for Mr Trump. Shortly afterwards he appeared on their show. That helped raise the profile of the hosts, as well as of tech podcasting in general. (For more than a year Mr Sacks was Mr Trump’s AI “czar”, even dressing the part by wearing a Russian fur hat at an “All-In” event.)

P7 Since then such superstar shows have gone from strength to strength. They take different approaches. The hosts of “All-In” delight in being anti-woke. They abhor what they call the “lamestream media” almost as much as they lampoon effective altruism (EA), a philosophical doctrine that they argue puts too much focus on the existential risks of AI. Mr Patel, in contrast, has said EA influenced him to start the “Dwarkesh Podcast” on ai. “TBPN” is not all bells and whistles. Its hosts have a deep knowledge of venture capital (VC), which they demystify with quips; they also work closely with media outlets and give airtime to their tech scoops.

P8 Helping them thrive is what would be called, in Silicon Valley jargon, a flywheel. Just before he hit the big time in 2024, Mr Patel tweeted his bank balance. It showed savings of $0.14. Then Mr Zuckerberg came onto the show and the advertising dollars poured in. As more prominent executives take part, not only do advertisers notice; so do potential customers, investors and jobseekers, raising the quality of the audience. “This is luxurious media with very valuable eyeballs,” says Josh Kaplan, boss of Smooth Media, which represents podcasters.

P9 The format is particularly well-suited to Silicon Valley, where founders invent stories for a living. They are venerated like the great men of history (Silicon Valley is still mostly a great manosphere). “We’re not journalists; we are enthusiasts,” says Patrick O’Shaughnessy, whose podcast, “Invest Like the Best”, has a strong following in VC circles. Firms such as Andreessen Horowitz have piled in too, creating podcasts to tout their portfolio companies to investors and themselves to founders. These offerings may be popular, but do not expect them to invest in the costly investigative work that uncovers frauds, such as that at Theranos, a health-tech startup, exposed by the Wall Street Journal in 2015.

P10 Instead, AI is at the centre of their discussions. Unlike many parts of America, they treat it as almost the only topic worth discussing. But this obsession has further pushed Silicon Valley into a “monoculture”, says David Senra, an influential podcaster. Recently he has been to tech dinners where conversation veers only between AI optimism and doomerism. “It sounds like they are all reading the same tweets,” he says.

P11 Lulu Cheng Meservey, a PR consultant to tech founders, reckons nine-tenths of people in tech speak only to other people in tech. Podcasts exacerbate that. As for using the format to transmit enthusiasm about AI to the outside world, it is not working. They may be well-meaning, she says, but “when a billionaire AI salesman is telling you whether to buy AI, you are taking that from someone who is entirely unrelatable to you.”

P12 It may get worse. Silicon Valley’s podcasters sprinkle their conversations with reverence for space missions, robots and other techno-wizardry. In an AIpocalypse, they would come out better off than most people. Mr Battelle calls it the “golden ticket on the rocket ship”. If all else fails, they can podcast from Mars. But will people still want to listen to them?

💡 媒体行话：valuable eyeballs 值钱的注意力；eyeball economy 注意力经济
长难句解析 · ANALYSIS

As more prominent executives take part, not only do advertisers notice; so do potential customers, investors and jobseekers, raising the quality of the audience.

从句As more prominent executives take part —— As 引导时间/原因：“随着……”
主句not only do advertisers notice; so do potential customers, investors and jobseekers —— not only 置首倒装（do advertisers notice）+ so do 倒装并列
短语raising the quality of the audience —— 分词作结果状语
考点not only do A do sth; so do B（A 如此，B 亦然）双重倒装是本文最值得仿写的句式；raising ... 收尾交代净效果。
PARA 9 · 讲解
参考译文 · TRANSLATION

这种形式尤其适合硅谷——在那里，创业者以编故事为生。他们像历史伟人一样被膜拜（硅谷至今仍基本是个“伟人男权圈”）。“我们不是记者；我们是爱好者。”帕特里克·奥肖内西说，他的播客“Invest Like the Best”在 VC 圈颇受欢迎。安德森·霍洛维茨等机构也蜂拥而入，做播客向投资者推介其投资组合公司、向创业者推销自己。这些节目或许受欢迎，但别指望它们投资昂贵的调查报道去揭穿欺诈——比如 2015 年《华尔街日报》曝光的健康科技初创公司 Theranos 那种。

本段词汇 · WORDS
veneratev.崇敬，膜拜
💡 be venerated like great men 被像伟人一样膜拜；名词 veneration
manospheren.男权话语圈（仿 blogosphere 造词）
💡 great manosphere 伟人男权圈：调侃硅谷“大佬叙事”；-sphere 后缀表话语生态
pile inphr.v.蜂拥而入
💡 firms have piled in 机构蜂拥入场；口语，含“扎堆、一哄而上”意味
toutv.兜售，极力推销
💡 tout sth to sb 向某人推销；负面色彩：吹嘘、兜售
长难句解析 · ANALYSIS

These offerings may be popular, but do not expect them to invest in the costly investigative work that uncovers frauds, such as that at Theranos, a health-tech startup, exposed by the Wall Street Journal in 2015.

主句These offerings may be popular, but do not expect them to invest in ... —— 先扬后抑：或许受欢迎，但别指望它们掏钱做调查报道
从句that uncovers frauds —— 定语从句修饰 investigative work
短语such as that at Theranos (= such as the fraud at Theranos) —— that 替代上文 frauds；后接同位语 + 过去分词补充
考点do not expect sb to do（别指望某人做）是委婉否定的经典句式；that 替代名词（= the fraud）避免重复，读时默补。
PARA 10 · 讲解
参考译文 · TRANSLATION

AI 是他们讨论的核心。与美国许多地方不同，他们视之为几乎唯一值得讨论的话题。但知名播客主大卫·森拉说，这种执念进一步把硅谷推向“单一文化”。最近他参加过一些科技晚宴，席间话题只在 AI 乐观论和末日论之间摇摆。“听起来就像他们都在读同样的推文。”他说。

本段词汇 · WORDS
monoculturen.单一文化（农业原义：单一种植）
💡 借喻思想/话语的单一化；反义 diversity/pluralism
obsessionn.执念，痴迷
💡 obsess 的名词；an obsession with 对……的执念
veer (between)v.（话题）在……之间摇摆
💡 veer 本义（车辆）转向；conversation veers between A and B 话题在两极间摆动
doomerismn.末日论（AI 语境：AI 末日论）

主句he has been to tech dinners —— 现在完成时：最近去过（强调近期经历）
从句where conversation veers only between AI optimism and doomerism —— where 引导定语从句修饰 dinners；veer between A and B（在两极间摇摆）
考点veer between A and B 描写话题/立场摇摆非常形象；only 一词道尽“话题之窄”，是单一文化论的关键证据句。
PARA 11 · 讲解
参考译文 · TRANSLATION

为科技创业者做公关的陆陈梅雪估计，科技圈九成人只跟圈内人说话。播客让这一情况雪上加霜。至于用这种形式向外界传递对 AI 的热情——并不奏效。她说，他们或许出于好意，但“当一个亿万富翁 AI 推销员告诉你该不该买 AI 时，你面对的是一个跟你毫无共鸣的人。”

本段词汇 · WORDS
reckonv.认为，估计（英式口语）
💡 = think/estimate；新闻转述常用 reckon that ...（省略 that）
transmitv.传递，传达
💡 transmit enthusiasm/message 传递热情/信息；比 spread 更正式
well-meaningadj.出于好意的（常含“但不顶用”）
💡 They may be well-meaning, but ... 他们或许好意，但……；常用让步框架
unrelatableadj.无法产生共鸣的，不可亲近的
💡 relatable 能让人产生共鸣的 → un- 否定；entirely unrelatable to you 跟你毫无共鸣
exacerbatev.加剧，恶化（= aggravate）
💡 与 004 篇同词复现：Podcasts exacerbate that 播客让这情况雪上加霜
长难句解析 · ANALYSIS

They may be well-meaning, she says, but “when a billionaire AI salesman is telling you whether to buy AI, you are taking that from someone who is entirely unrelatable to you.”

主句They may be well-meaning, ... but ... —— may 表让步：“或许出于好意，但是……”（先扬后抑框架）
从句when a billionaire AI salesman is telling you whether to buy AI —— when 引导情境条件
从句you are taking that from someone who is entirely unrelatable to you —— 主句 + who 定语从句收尾点题
考点may be well-meaning, but ...（好意但不顶用）是批评类写作的柔和起手式；be unrelatable to sb（与某人无共鸣）是全文点题词。
PARA 12 · 讲解
参考译文 · TRANSLATION

情况还可能更糟。硅谷播客主播的谈话中，处处洒满对太空任务、机器人和其他科技魔法的敬畏。真到 AI 末日，他们也会比大多数人过得好。巴特尔称之为“火箭船上的金票”。就算一切都完了，他们还能在火星上录播客。但——人们还愿意听吗？

本段词汇 · WORDS
sprinkle (with)v.洒满，点缀（比喻）
💡 sprinkle conversations with reverence 谈话中点缀着敬畏；sprinkle sth with sth
reverencen.敬畏，崇敬
💡 reverence for 对……的敬畏；动词 revere；与 theme 词 venerate 同根呼应
techno-wizardryn.科技魔法（戏谑）
💡 wizardry 巫术、魔法；techno-wizardry 调侃技术崇拜

长难句解析 · ANALYSIS

In an AIpocalypse, they would come out better off than most people.

短语In an AIpocalypse —— 造词（AI + apocalypse）：AI 末日；作者的自嘲式幽默
主句they would come out better off than most people —— would 表虚拟推演；come out better off（结局更好）
考点AIpocalypse 这类合成造词（blend）是杂志标题语言的调味剂；come out + 比较级（结果是……）用于推演结局。结尾一问“But will people still want to listen to them?”戛然而止——设问收尾值得在写作中模仿。

Background

Context that helps you understand the article.

硅谷播客权力榜

文中四大节目各有生态位：TBPN（深夜秀式日播，2026 年被 OpenAI 收购）；All-In（四位富豪主持，横跨科技与 MAGA，2024 年为特朗普筹款）；Dwarkesh Podcast（26 岁主播与 AI 大佬深谈数小时，被称为“AI 研究大祭司”）；Lex Fridman（超长访谈）。播客呈幂律分布：少数节目聚集顶级嘉宾与广告，强者恒强。2024 年大选是转折点——政客绕过传统媒体直接上播客发声。

回音室、有效利他与末日论

文章批评的核心是“圈内自交”：主播与嘉宾共享技术乐观主义，互称 besties、合租房子、企业收购节目。AI 话语在乐观论（AI will save us）与末日论（doomerism，AI 会毁灭我们）两极摇摆，形成“单一文化”。有效利他主义（EA）是被反复争论的对象——All-In 嘲讽它高估 AI 生存风险，Dwarkesh 则承认受其影响。传统媒体与硅谷的裂痕（互指“反美”与“酸葡萄”）是播客兴起的背景。
