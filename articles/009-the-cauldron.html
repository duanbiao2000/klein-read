Fancy a tour through the Middle East?

The Economist — 2026-10-08

P1 By some accounts it was the most expensive party in history. To commemorate the 2,500th anniversary of the founding of the Persian empire, Muhammad Reza Pahlavi, the last shah of Iran, invited kings, queens, emperors and presidents to celebrate in a lavish encampment amid the ancient ruins of Persepolis. Guests sipped champagne from Moët & Chandon and wine from Château Lafite Rothschild. They feasted on food from Maxim’s of Paris and gawked at 50,000 imported songbirds. (The creatures all died in the heat.)

P2 Iranians were outraged by the excess. “We can hardly offer them bread and radishes,” came the shah’s tone-deaf retort. For Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini, the architect and father of Iran’s revolution, the party was the “devil’s festival”. It marked the beginning of the end of the shah’s rule. Seven years later, the shah fled from the country. Those close to him were executed. Even his horse was publicly tortured and shot. The party was over.

P3 In a previous book, Simon Sebag Montefiore, a British historian, took on the history of the world. “The Cauldron”, his latest work, is somehow even more ambitious: it tackles the history of the Middle East since 1900. You might wonder whether there is much new to be said on the subject. Rich histories of the Arabs abound. The conflict between the Israelis and Palestinians has generated, by Mr Montefiore’s own admission, “a veritable industry” of analysis. Countless tomes pick through the diplomacy, wars and revolutions that have shaped the region.

P4 Mr Montefiore’s contribution distinguishes itself in a number of ways. He takes on the entire middle, including Turkey, Iran, Israel and the Arab countries: this is a surprisingly rare approach. More than that, he brings the reader to the present day, analysing events that continue to unfold. He aims to “lighten the heavy matters of religion, geopolitics and ideology” by telling this history through “human stories, often those of rulers”. And Mr Montefiore aspires to restore “some balance” to the way those in the West look at the Middle East. None is an easy task.

P5 Yet the sheer scope of “The Cauldron” is breathtaking. He whizzes around the region, from Mustafa Kemal Ataturk battling Allied troops in 1915 to Riyadh, as it was seized by Abdulaziz bin Abdul Rahman al-Saud to establish the kingdom of Saudi Arabia in 1932. Then the book marches onwards, through the founding of Israel, the Iranian revolution, the horror of Sudan’s civil war and the bloodshed of the past three years.

P6 The breadth of the book inevitably means depth is sacrificed. The Armenian genocide of 1915-16 is dealt with in less than a page. But Mr Montefiore also manages to include a rich array of details that bring the grand sweep to life (for example the fact that, after America, Iran was the second country to recognise the new state of Israel; or the comment by Gamal Abdel Nasser of Egypt to Leonid Brezhnev that if he were Israel’s leader: “I’d never give up the Occupied Territories.”)

P7 Given just how many of those “human stories” Mr Montefiore races through, he could perhaps have taken a leaf out of one of Dame Jilly Cooper’s tomes and included a cast of characters. It would certainly have been colourful. “The Cauldron” bubbles with personalities, including King Farouk of Egypt, nicknamed “Fat Fucker” by the CIA; Mohammad Mosaddegh, the “pyjama-clad valetudinarian”; and Abolhassan Banisadr, first president of the new Iranian republic, with the “clownish moustache, oversized spectacles and sharp suits of a Nouvelle Vague film director”.

P8 Balance is trickier. He brings it first by writing about the region directly through the prism of its member states, rather than imperialists or invaders. At a time when America is stepping back from its role as the dominant power in the Middle East, and no country looks willing or able to replace it, that is a worthwhile endeavour.

P9 It is especially hard to write about history as it is still being made. His analysis of the attacks of October 7th 2023 and Israel’s response—“the largest, longest and most unrestrained war in its history”—will strike many readers as eminently reasonable. And yet no doubt some will find his judgments too generous to Israel, while other readers consider him too sympathetic to the Palestinians.

P10 But where he is spot on is in the significance he ascribes to the Iranian revolution. The extent to which he weaves Iran into the history of the Middle East, rather than treating it in isolation as so many others do, and highlighting its importance not just to the region but to the world, is one of the book’s signal achievements. He likens its impact to that of the French and Russian revolutions of 1789 and 1917, arguing that, like them, it “changed the world”. The current maelstrom in the Middle East bears out that judgment.

PARA 4 · 讲解
参考译文 · TRANSLATION

蒙蒂菲奥里的这部作品以多种方式脱颖而出。他把整个中东都纳入视野，包括土耳其、伊朗、以色列和阿拉伯国家；如此全面的写法出人意料地少见。不止如此，他把读者带到当下，分析仍在发展的事件。他希望通过“人的故事，往往是统治者的故事”，来减轻宗教、地缘政治和意识形态这些沉重议题的阅读负担。蒙蒂菲奥里还力求为西方看待中东的方式“恢复某种平衡”。这些任务都不轻松。

长难句解析 · ANALYSIS

Given just how many of those “human stories” Mr Montefiore races through, he could perhaps have taken a leaf out of one of Dame Jilly Cooper’s tomes and included a cast of characters.

短语Given just how many of those “human stories” Mr Montefiore races through —— 句首 Given 表“考虑到”；how many 引导嵌入式感叹/疑问结构，作 Given 的宾语
主句he could perhaps have taken a leaf out of ... and included a cast of characters —— 主句用 could have + 过去分词提出事后建议；两个动词短语由 and 并列
短语one of Dame Jilly Cooper’s tomes —— one of + 复数名词；tome 是大部头著作
考点could perhaps have done 表示“本可以……（但实际没有）”，常用于委婉批评；take a leaf out of 是“借鉴某人的做法”，不是字面“撕下一页”。
PARA 8 · 讲解
参考译文 · TRANSLATION

平衡更难把握。首先，他直接从中东各成员国的视角书写该地区，而不是透过帝国主义者或入侵者的棱镜。如今，美国正逐渐淡出其作为中东主导力量的角色，而没有哪个国家看起来愿意或有能力取而代之；在这样的时刻，这种写法值得肯定。

本段词汇 · WORDS
trickyadj.棘手的；难以处理的
💡 balance is trickier 指做到客观平衡比提出目标更难；tricky question 棘手的问题
prismn.棱镜；（比喻）观察事物的视角
💡 through the prism of member states 透过成员国视角；也常见 through the lens of...
member staten.phr.成员国
💡 常用于国际组织；此处指中东地区的各个国家
step back fromphr.v.从……中后退；减少参与或影响
💡 America is stepping back from its role 指美国正收缩其在该地区的主导角色
endeavourn.努力；值得投入的事业
💡 英式拼法 endeavour，美式为 endeavor；a worthwhile endeavour 值得付出的努力
长难句解析 · ANALYSIS

At a time when America is stepping back from its role as the dominant power in the Middle East, and no country looks willing or able to replace it, that is a worthwhile endeavour.

短语At a time when America is stepping back from its role ... —— 介词短语引出背景；when 引导定语从句修饰 a time
从句and no country looks willing or able to replace it —— 与前一背景并列；it 指 America’s role / dominant power position
主句that is a worthwhile endeavour —— 主句对前文所述的写作方式作出评价；that 指从成员国视角书写中东
考点句首背景拉长后，主句直到 that is 才出现。it 的指代是“美国在中东的主导角色”，而不是美国这个国家本身；可用这种结构先交代时代背景，再给出判断。
PARA 9 · 讲解
参考译文 · TRANSLATION

历史仍在书写时，尤其难以下笔。他对 2023 年 10 月 7 日袭击以及以色列回应的分析——“这是其历史上规模最大、持续最久、也最不受约束的一场战争”——在许多读者看来会显得极其合理。然而，毫无疑问，有些人会认为他的判断对以色列过于宽容；另一些读者则觉得他对巴勒斯坦人过于同情。

本段词汇 · WORDS
as it is still being madeclause在历史仍不断发生、尚未定论之时
💡 history is still being made 是常见表达，指重大事件仍在发生、历史结论尚未尘埃落定
unrestrainedadj.不受约束的；毫无节制的
💡 an unrestrained war 指缺少约束、规模和烈度都很大的战争；动词 restrain 是“约束”
strike sb asphr.给某人留下……印象；让某人觉得……
💡 will strike many readers as reasonable = 会让许多读者觉得合理；strike 不表示“打击”
eminentlyadv.非常；极其（适合或明显）
💡 eminently reasonable 极其合理；常与 suitable, qualified, sensible 等形容词搭配
generous toadj.phr.对……过于宽容；对……评价偏厚
💡 too generous to Israel 在政治评论中意为判断对以色列过于宽厚，不是“慷慨捐赠”
长难句解析 · ANALYSIS

His analysis of the attacks of October 7th 2023 and Israel’s response—“the largest, longest and most unrestrained war in its history”—will strike many readers as eminently reasonable.

主句His analysis ... will strike many readers as eminently reasonable —— 主干：他的分析会让许多读者觉得极其合理
短语of the attacks ... and Israel’s response —— of 短语说明 analysis 的对象
短语—“the largest, longest and most unrestrained war in its history”— —— 破折号内为插入评价；三个最高级并列，强调战争规模、持续时间和缺少约束
短语strike many readers as eminently reasonable —— strike sb as... 表“让某人觉得……”；as 后接评价
考点主句骨架是 His analysis will strike many readers as reasonable；破折号中的插入语承载作者的强烈定性。评论时要区分事实概述与评价性用语。
PARA 10 · 讲解
参考译文 · TRANSLATION

但他判断最准确之处，在于他赋予伊朗革命的重要性。他没有像许多人那样孤立地看待伊朗，而是把它编织进中东史，并凸显其不仅对本地区、也对世界的重要性——这是本书最突出的成就之一。他把伊朗革命的影响比作 1789 年法国革命和 1917 年俄国革命，认为它们一样“改变了世界”。如今中东的巨大动荡印证了这一判断。

本段词汇 · WORDS
spot onadj.phr.完全正确的；判断精准的
💡 where he is spot on = 他判断最准确的地方；口语化但常见于评论
significancen.重要性；意义
💡 the significance of an event 某事件的重要性；形容词 significant
ascribe sth to sb/sthphr.v.认为某人或某事具有……；把……归因于
💡 the significance he ascribes to the revolution = 他赋予这场革命的重要性；结构为 ascribe A to B
weave A into Bphr.v.把 A 编织进 B；将 A 融入 B
💡 weave Iran into the history 把伊朗纳入中东史叙事；比 simply include 更有连贯交织的画面
bear outphr.v.证实；印证
💡 evidence bears out a claim 证据印证某个说法；此处指当下局势支持作者对革命影响的判断
长难句解析 · ANALYSIS

He likens its impact to that of the French and Russian revolutions of 1789 and 1917, arguing that, like them, it “changed the world”.

主句He likens its impact to that of the French and Russian revolutions —— 主干：他把伊朗革命的影响与法国、俄国革命相提并论；that 代替 impact
短语of 1789 and 1917 —— 年份分别对应法国革命与俄国革命
短语arguing that, like them, it “changed the world” —— 现在分词短语补充说明比较后的论点；that 引导 arguing 的内容从句，like them 表示“像它们一样”
考点liken A to B 是“把 A 比作 B”；that of... 中 that 代替前面的 impact，避免重复。作者以三场革命并列，支撑“伊朗革命影响全球”的判断。

波斯波利斯庆典：奢华背后的政治落差

文中提到的庆典由伊朗末代沙阿穆罕默德·礼萨·巴列维举办，地点在古代波斯帝国都城波斯波利斯遗址附近。它以纪念帝国历史为名，却因铺张排场与普通民众的生活处境形成强烈反差。霍梅尼称其为“魔鬼的节日”，评论借这场宴会说明君主制与民众之间不断扩大的距离。

《大熔炉》的地理与叙事范围

这本书把土耳其、伊朗、以色列和阿拉伯国家放在同一幅区域史图景中，并一路写到仍在发展的当代事件。文章特别肯定它不把伊朗孤立处理，而是将伊朗革命放入更广阔的中东乃至世界历史脉络；同时也提醒，覆盖面越大，单个事件获得的篇幅和深度越可能受限。

Useful Expressions

You can reuse these in exam writing.

mark the beginning of the end

标志着……开始走向终结

📌 The party marked the beginning of the end of the shah’s rule. 可用于转折点：The scandal marked the beginning of the end of his career.

a veritable industry

名副其实的一个产业；规模大到形成“产业”

📌 用夸张方式形容某类活动或内容极为繁多：The conflict has generated a veritable industry of analysis.

bring the grand sweep to life

让宏大的历史全景鲜活起来

📌 写作中可用来说明具体例子如何让宏观叙事更生动：Personal stories bring a broad historical account to life.

take a leaf out of someone’s book

借鉴某人的做法；效仿某人

📌 可用于提出建议：The city could take a leaf out of its neighbour’s book.

through the prism of

透过……的视角；从……角度观察

📌 The review praises a history told through the prism of the region’s own states. 也可换用 through the lens of。

bear out a judgment

印证某项判断

📌 The latest evidence bears out that judgment. bear out 后接 claim, theory, prediction 或 judgment。
